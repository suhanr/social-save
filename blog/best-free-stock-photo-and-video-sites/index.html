Best Free Stock Photo and Video Sites (and How to Read Their Licences)

If you need images or clips you can actually reuse, free stock libraries are the safer route. These are well-known places to start, plus a licence checklist and tips for choosing files.

Where to look

  • Pexels: photos and videos from contributors, with its own licence.
  • Pixabay: photos, illustrations, vectors and videos under the Pixabay licence.
  • Unsplash: well known for high-quality photographs.
  • Wikimedia Commons: media under open licences or in the public domain, each file with its own terms.

Each site writes its own licence, and licences change over time. Always read the one shown on the page you download from, not a summary you remember.

What to check in a licence

  • Commercial use. Can you use it in a product, advert or paid work?
  • Attribution. Do you need to credit the creator?
  • Modifications. Can you crop, edit or remix it?
  • Resale. Usually you cannot sell the file on its own.
  • People, logos and artwork. A free licence may not cover the rights of people, brands or buildings that appear in the picture.

Choosing a good file

  • Resolution. Pick a size larger than you need so you can crop.
  • Format. Photos are usually JPG; videos are usually MP4.
  • Relevance. Search with simple words, then filter by orientation or colour.
  • Originality. Popular images appear everywhere, so look beyond the first page if you want something distinctive.

Photos or video?

Photos are easier to find and use. Video clips are larger, so check the resolution and format before you download. Our guide to video formats explains what to pick.

Keep a record

Write down where each file came from, the page address and the licence name, and keep that note with the project. If someone later asks where an image came from, you will have the answer.

Frequently asked questions

Is free stock media safe to use commercially?

Many licences allow it, but not all, and some limit pictures of people or brands. Read the licence for each file.

Do I have to credit the photographer?

Sometimes. Check the licence; even when it is optional, crediting is good practice.

Can I edit a stock photo?

Most licences allow edits, but check the specific terms.
