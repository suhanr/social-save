Is It Legal to Download Social Media Videos?

Short answer: it depends. Four things matter: who owns the video, what the platform's terms say, what you plan to do with it, and the law where you live. This is general information, not legal advice.

Who owns the video?

In most countries copyright arises automatically when someone creates a video, photo or piece of music. Posting it on a platform does not hand ownership to the platform or to viewers. The creator, or whoever they licensed it to, decides what others may do with it.

Platform terms are a separate layer

Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube each have terms of service. Some allow downloads only through their own features. Breaking a platform's terms is not automatically a crime, but it can lead to a lost account, and it is a different issue from copyright.

Exceptions differ by country

Some countries allow limited private copying, quoting for criticism or news, or educational use. These rules are narrow and differ from place to place, and a rule that applies in one country may not apply in another. Treat them as exceptions to check, not as a general permission.

Music, people and brands

A video can contain several layers of rights: the footage, the music, the people who appear in it and any logos or artwork. A creator may own the footage but only have a licence for the music, which is why a clip can be fine on one platform and blocked on another.

Creative Commons and public domain

Some creators publish under Creative Commons licences. These allow specific uses, such as sharing with credit, but each licence has conditions. Public domain works have no copyright restrictions, but check that the work really is in the public domain before you assume it.

A simple checklist

  1. Is it your own content? Then you are on the safest ground.
  2. Does it carry a licence, such as Creative Commons? Read the exact licence terms.
  3. Do you have the creator's permission? Keep it in writing.
  4. Is it only for private viewing? Then the risk is lower, but check the platform's terms.
  5. Are you unsure? Do not reupload it, or ask the creator first.

What if someone asks you to remove a video?

Take it down promptly and tell them it is done. Copyright holders can send takedown notices to platforms and sites, and ignoring a polite request makes a bigger problem out of a small one.

Frequently asked questions

Is it illegal to download a video just to watch it later?

It depends on the content, the platform's terms and the law where you live. Private viewing is generally lower risk than redistribution, but this is not legal advice.

Can I repost a video if I give credit?

Credit alone is not permission. You need the creator's permission or a licence that allows reposting.

Does a free downloader make downloading legal?

No. A tool does not change who owns the content or what the platform's terms say.
