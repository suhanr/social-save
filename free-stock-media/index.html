Why use stock media

Content you find on social media usually belongs to its creator. Stock libraries are different: their files are offered under licences that say what you may do with them, which makes them a safer choice for blogs, presentations, videos and design work.

Read the licence every time

  • Check whether commercial use is allowed.
  • Check whether attribution is required.
  • Check what you may edit or combine.
  • Do not assume a free licence covers the people, logos or artwork in a picture.

Photos and videos

Photos are small and easy to reuse. Video clips are larger, so check the resolution and format first; MP4 vs WebM vs MOV explains the common choices.

How to choose good stock media

  • Search with simple words, then narrow by orientation or colour.
  • Pick a resolution larger than you need, so you can crop.
  • Prefer files with a clear licence on the download page.
  • Avoid images with recognisable people or brands unless the licence clearly covers them.

If a stock download does not work

  1. Check that you pasted the link to a specific photo or video page.
  2. Try another file from the same source to see if the problem is with one item.
  3. Availability can vary depending on the source and media type.

Frequently asked questions

What does free stock media mean?

Free stock media is photography and video offered under a licence that allows reuse, often including commercial use, without paying per file. Terms differ between sites, so read the licence for each download.

Do I need to credit the creator?

Sometimes. Some licences require attribution and others do not. Check the licence on the page you download from.

Can I use stock media in a commercial project?

Many licences allow it, but not all, and some limit how you may use pictures of people or brands. Read the licence before you publish.

What is the difference between social media content and stock media?

Social media content generally belongs to its creator, so reuse needs permission. Stock media is published to be reused under a stated licence.
